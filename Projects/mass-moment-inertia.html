Experimental dynamics / Method validation

B-2 mass moment of inertia

Torsional vibration and pendulum testing | Cal Poly San Luis Obispo

Dynamics Torsional Vibration Physical Pendulum Experimental Validation Uncertainty Analysis

Working with Bryan Figueroa, I measured the mass moment of inertia of a B-2 Spirit model about its roll and yaw axes. We first tested both methods on a rectangular wood board with known theoretical inertia, then used the better validated method for the aircraft model.

01

Validate the measurement

Known geometry before complex geometry

The B-2 model has a complex mass distribution, so no simple geometric equation could provide a trustworthy reference. A uniform wood board supplied that reference. Its dimensions and mass produced theoretical inertia values about the y and z axes that could be compared directly with experiment.

We tested the board with torsional vibration and a physical pendulum. This sequence separated problems in the measurement method from the unknown inertia of the aircraft model.

0.20235 kg·m²Wood board theoretical y-axis inertia
0.20425 kg·m²Wood board theoretical z-axis inertia
2 methodsTorsional vibration and pendulum swing
02

Torsion method

Consistent periods and close agreement with theory

The torsion fixture suspended the test article from wires and allowed small angular oscillations. The measured period, wire spacing, and suspension height produced the experimental inertia.

The wood board result differed from theory by 7.66% about the y axis and only 0.93% about the z axis. That agreement supported using torsional vibration as the primary method for the B-2 model.

03

Pendulum method

A useful check with higher sensitivity

The pendulum method suspended the test article about an offset pivot. Its inertia calculation used the oscillation period and the measured distance from the pivot to the center of gravity.

The z-axis result stayed within 3.56% of theory, but the y-axis result missed theory by 13.85%. The equation subtracts two similar terms, so small errors in period or center-of-gravity location become much larger errors in the final inertia.

13.85%Board y-axis error versus theory
3.56%Board z-axis error versus theory
~10%Inertia error from a 1% period error
Sensitivity: roughly 80% of the two terms in the pendulum equation cancel. Timing more oscillations, using a photogate, and locating the center of gravity by balancing would reduce the dominant uncertainty.
04

B-2 model results

Roll and yaw inertia from measured oscillation

The validated torsion method gave a roll inertia of 0.05476 kg·m² and a yaw inertia of 0.09325 kg·m². The larger yaw value matched the expected behavior of a wide, flat body with more mass distributed away from the vertical axis.

The pendulum test gave a yaw inertia of 0.05894 kg·m², 36.79% below the torsion result. Based on the board validation and the pendulum method’s sensitivity, we selected the torsion value as the more reliable estimate.

05

Engineering takeaway

A measured number needs a validated method

The wood board made the experiment defensible. It showed that the torsion setup could reproduce a known inertia before we applied it to the B-2 model.

The disagreement between yaw methods also showed why repeatability alone is not enough. A stable period can still produce a biased result when the equation magnifies small setup errors.
