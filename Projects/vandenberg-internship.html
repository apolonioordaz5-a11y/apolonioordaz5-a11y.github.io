Internship · Vandenberg Space Force Base · 2026

Rebuilt a WWII P-40’s propellers, from rust to ready.

The base’s P-40 Warhawk display had lost its propellers to rust. I rebuilt them, from a 3D scan to CNC-machined, steel-reinforced, fiberglass-finished hardware.

Role
Rapid Prototyping & Manufacturing Engineering Intern
Team
TDDEC, U.S. Space Force
Project
Individual
Year
2026

The problem

The steel struts inside the propellers had rusted away, and the blades fell off the nose cone.

What I did

Scanned a surviving blade, machined new ones, reinforced them with stainless steel and fiberglass, and rebuilt the nose-cone support.

The result

A stronger, longer-lasting propeller assembly that restores the display’s original look, now heading to paint and reinstallation.

Before and after

How I built it

  1. 01

    Scan

    3D-scanned a reference propeller to capture its exact shape for machining.

  2. 02

    Machine

    Cut each new blade on a CNC gantry router.

  3. 03

    Reinforce

    Embedded stainless-steel rods and wrapped the blades in fiberglass so they last outdoors.

  4. 04

    Assemble

    Fit the blades into the rebuilt nose cone and checked their alignment.

Skills I used

  • 3D scanning
  • CNC gantry routing
  • Composite layup
  • Stainless-steel reinforcement
  • Assembly & alignment
  • Restoration planning

Life at TDDEC

TDDEC is a U.S. Space Force flight that rapidly repairs and builds mission-critical training equipment. Over the internship I learned more than 20 machines across the shop.

Engineering details
  • Verified nose-cone and propeller alignment during assembly.
  • Evaluated the propeller support and internal reinforcement.
  • Validated material choices for long-term outdoor exposure.

Next steps: prime and paint, reinstall on the aircraft, and pass a safety inspection.
