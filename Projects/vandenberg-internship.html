Internship / Rapid prototyping

Vandenberg Space Force Base

Rapid Prototyping & Manufacturing Engineering Intern | Summer 2026

Requirements Rapid Prototyping Fabrication Manufacturing Technical Communication
Ø 1.250 4.500
Process illustration / representative

At Vandenberg Space Force Base, I worked in a professional machine-shop environment to translate documented requirements into functional training aids and prototypes. The internship connected engineering intent, fabrication decisions, and clear communication around real hardware.

01

Engineering responsibility

Move from a stated need to something testable

The work began with documented requirements rather than a finished part definition. My responsibility was to understand the intended use, convert that need into a practical hardware approach, and fabricate a result that could be evaluated in context.

That process required decisions about geometry, material use, manufacturability, and how to communicate changes as the prototype developed.

02

Prototype workflow

Requirements, build, evaluate, refine

DefineClarify the requirement and the hardware’s intended use
FabricateChoose a practical sequence and produce the prototype
EvaluateReview function and use the result to guide refinement
Constraint: The portfolio describes the engineering process without presenting restricted work details or inventing project-specific performance claims.
03

Manufacturing perspective

Design choices become shop operations

Working in the shop reinforced that a design is only complete when it can be produced, inspected, and used. Features that appear simple in a model still affect setup, tool access, operation order, and the time required to make a reliable part.

The experience strengthened the feedback loop between how I define hardware and how I expect it to be built.

04

What I carried forward

Technical competence includes communication

The internship developed my ability to work from requirements, make fabrication-aware decisions, and communicate around physical results. It gave me a stronger standard for what it means to move an engineering idea into functional hardware.
