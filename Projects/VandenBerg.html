Baja SAE / Manufacturing

Rear Suspension Manufacturing

Cal Poly Racing | Rear Suspension Team | 2025–Present

Manual Mill Manual Lathe Engineering Drawings GD&T Inspection
Suspension architecture / representative diagram

As a member of Cal Poly Racing’s Baja SAE rear suspension team, I manufacture components on manual mills and lathes from engineering drawings. The work demands careful setup, dimensional control, and communication between the design intent and the finished hardware.

01

Team responsibility

Turn released drawings into reliable hardware

My role sits at the point where suspension geometry becomes a physical part. I interpret drawing views, dimensions, tolerances, and GD&T before planning a machining sequence that preserves critical references.

Each setup has to support the next operation, provide usable inspection surfaces, and produce a component that fits the larger assembly.

02

Manufacturing workflow

Plan, machine, inspect, communicate

PlanRead the drawing and identify critical datums, dimensions, and setups
MachineUse manual mill and lathe operations to create the specified geometry
InspectCheck critical features and resolve discrepancies with the team
03

Engineering judgment

Manufacturability is part of the design loop

Hands-on fabrication makes drawing clarity, tool access, datum selection, and operation order tangible. A feature can be valid in CAD but still create unnecessary setup risk or inspection difficulty in the shop.

Working with the rear suspension team has strengthened my ability to give manufacturing feedback early and to treat the drawing, process, and part as one connected engineering system.

Portfolio note: This case study focuses on my manufacturing process and responsibility without inventing dimensions or performance results that are not documented in the project material.
